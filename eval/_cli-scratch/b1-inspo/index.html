How a voice gets cut

booth to catalogue

  1. The speaker sits a session

    One booth, one supervising engineer, and a script written against the range you need rather than a generic passage. The speaker is in the room and reads the terms before the first take, not after.

  2. We cut the model in-house

    Training runs on our own hardware against that session and no other audio. When it is done, the speaker auditions their own voice and can send it back to be cut again.

  3. You direct it, they hold it

    You get the voice, the direction controls, and a written licence. The speaker keeps a revocation they can exercise themselves, and every render carries provenance metadata on the way out.

Consent is the product

Voice cloning goes wrong in three places, so the terms are written against those three and are the same terms for every entry in the catalogue.

Provenance
Every voice names the session it came from. Nothing enters the catalogue from scraped audiobooks, podcasts or films, and a voice with no traceable session does not get cut.
Revocation
The speaker can withdraw their voice from the catalogue at any time from their own account. Withdrawal stops new renders immediately; existing licences enter a wind-down we publish in full inside the terms.
Declaration
Renders leave with signed provenance metadata attached. Strip it and the licence lapses. We would rather lose the work than ship a voice that cannot be identified as cut.

Full terms and the speaker agreement are published before you are asked for anything.
