The Slow Web

Manifesto · MMXXVI · Edition One

We are done with the scroll.

A manifesto for the people who closed the tab before it finished loading, who muted the autoplay, who left the group chat on read — and meant it.

The web was sold to us as a library. It became a casino. Every page is a slot machine tuned to the same variable reward: the next thing, the new ping, the little red dot that means someone thought of you for half a second.

We are not asking to go back. We are asking to arrive. To read one thing and finish it. To write one thing and mean it. To close the laptop and feel that the hour was ours.

What follows is not a product, a platform, or a protocol. It is a posture. Five tenets we hold, and a line where you can hold them with us.

The Five Tenets

  1. 01

    One thing at a time.

    A page is a page. It is not a feed. It does not refresh. It does not have a "next post" button. When you finish reading, you are finished — and that is the design.

  2. 02

    No infinite scroll.

    The bottom of the page is a feature, not a bug. Reaching it is a small mercy. We design for the moment the reader looks up, not the moment they look down again.

  3. 03

    No engagement metrics.

    No likes. No views. No streaks. No little red dots. A piece of writing is not improved by the count of people who glanced at it. We refuse to measure attention in fractions of a second.

  4. 04

    Slow is a feature.

    Pages may take a moment to load. Letters may take a day to be answered. We do not apologise for this. The pause is where the meaning gets in.

  5. 05

    Close the tab.

    The highest compliment a reader can pay this page is to close it, walk away, and remember what it said. We are not building a return audience. We are building a memory.
