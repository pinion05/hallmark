01 · the instrument

four parts, one machine.

no presets, no character packs, no stock narrators. vesper is the bare apparatus — you bring the voice, the consent, and the lines.

  • CAPTURE

    ninety seconds, any room.

    record on a phone, a podcast mic, a field recorder. vesper aligns the take, strips the room, and keeps the breath. background under −45 dB is left alone.

  • EMBED

    one speaker vector.

    the take becomes a 512-dimension embedding — a fingerprint of timbre, cadence, and pitch contour. the source audio is discarded once the vector is signed.

  • RENDER

    new lines, same person.

    type a line, get it back in their voice at 24 kHz. emphasis, pauses, and breaths are markup, not magic — you control them in plain text.

  • WATERMARK

    every file says where it came from.

    an inaudible signature rides inside every render and survives re-encoding. paste any vesper clip into the verifier and it names the key that made it.

02 · how it runs

from a take to a line, in four steps.

  1. 1.0

    get a signed consent link.

    the speaker confirms the clone from their own device. no link, no vector — vesper will not learn a voice that hasn’t said yes.

  2. 2.0

    upload ninety seconds.

    one continuous take reads cleanest, but vesper will stitch up to six short clips into a single vector.

  3. 3.0

    render from plain text.

    vesper render --voice rx-09 "tuesday, half past four." returns a wav in about a second. pauses and emphasis live in the markup, not a black box.

  4. 4.0

    ship it watermarked.

    every file leaves with its signature attached. revoke the key and new renders stop the same minute.

03 · consent

a voice is a person. we treat it like one.

most voice tools ask you to tick a box swearing you have permission. vesper asks the speaker instead. the consent link is signed on their device, scoped to the projects you name, and expires on the date you set.

we don’t sell voices, we don’t pool them into a shared model, and we don’t keep your source audio once the vector is built. when a speaker revokes, their vector is deleted and every render carrying their watermark can be traced and pulled.

— built in lisbon by six people who’d want the same rules applied to their own voices.

04 · the questions you actually have

answered like a person.

can i clone a voice that isn’t mine?

only with their signed consent. the speaker confirms from their own device before a vector is built — there is no form-tick shortcut, and there is no “trust me” mode.

what happens to my recording?

it becomes a 512-dimension vector and the original wav is deleted within the hour. the vector can rebuild the voice; it cannot rebuild the words you recorded.

is there an api, or just a console?

both. the console is for one-off renders; the http api and the vesper cli are for pipelines. same auth, same watermark, same revoke.

what languages does it render?

english, portuguese, spanish, french, and german today. the timbre carries across all five from a single english take — accent stays the speaker’s own.
