A manifesto · Edition 01 · Drafted at low tide, March 2026

The web does not need to be faster. It needs to be kept.

We are a small collective of writers, designers, and engineers who make pages meant to be read once and remembered — not refreshed, not optimised for the next thirty seconds of attention. This is what we hold to.

Read what we mean ↓

What we mean by slow.

Slow is not lazy. A slow page is one that did its thinking before you arrived, so you don’t have to wait for it to think while you’re looking at it.

It loads in one breath. It does not stutter, reflow, or beg you to accept anything. The words sit still long enough to be read by a person who is tired at the end of a long day.

The fast web treats your attention as a resource to extract. The slow web treats it as a guest to look after.

Five things we commit to.

  1. 01

    One page, one weight.

    Every page ships under the weight of a single photograph. No tracker bundles, no framework we didn’t need, no fourth font. If it doesn’t earn its kilobytes, it doesn’t board.

  2. 02

    It works without the script.

    The reading comes first and arrives in plain HTML. JavaScript is the layer that makes a good page better, never the thing standing between you and the words.

  3. 03

    No interruption tax.

    No cookie wall on arrival, no newsletter sheet over your first paragraph, no countdown. You came to read. We let you read, and we ask afterwards, quietly, if at all.

  4. 04

    Built to outlast its framework.

    We write for a page that still opens in ten years. Plain markup, owned fonts, links that resolve. We would rather be readable and dated than fashionable and broken.

  5. 05

    A human answers.

    When you write to us, a person we can name reads it and writes back. We will not put a machine in front of the door and call it support.

And what we refuse.

We will not measure you by the second.
A reader is not a session. Time on page is not a verdict on the writing.
We will not autoplay anything.
Sound and motion start when you ask for them, and stop when you leave.
We will not borrow your battery.
No animation runs that you didn’t come to see. The page rests when it is still.
We will not pretend to be paper.
This is a screen, made well for a screen. We honour print without imitating it.

Make a page worth the wait, then make it worth no wait at all.

One letter, four times a year. No tracking pixels — we count signatories by hand.
